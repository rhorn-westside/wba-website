## The shape of an elementary day

Elementary classes are self-contained. One teacher carries a class through Bible, reading, English, math, science and history, which means that teacher sees the whole child — the reader who is flying and the same child stuck on subtraction — rather than a forty-minute slice. Music and fine arts are taught by specialists.

Because the school is small, grades are paired in some classrooms. This is a feature more often than a compromise: younger students hear the material a year early, older ones consolidate it by hearing it again, and the teacher carries the same students for two years and loses no time in September getting to know them.

## Reading

Reading instruction is phonics-first and explicit. Children are taught the code, then given real books.

## Homework

Elementary homework goes home on a weekly schedule sheet, one per grade, listing each day's work. Depending on the grade the sheet also carries a memory verse, a spelling list, a place for parent initials, and a note from the teacher.

Parents can view or print the current week's sheet without signing in.

## Grading

Elementary grading follows the same structure as junior and senior high, with two differences suited to younger students: teachers may include an **Effort and Participation** category, and Art and PE are marked E / S / NI / U rather than given numeric grades.

## Starting kindergarten

K5 students must be **five years old by October 1**. Exceptions are made on a case-by-case basis — call the office if your child's birthday falls close to the line.

All new students undergo assessment testing during enrollment.
